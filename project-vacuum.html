Overview & Ownership

Turning failure into a validated first-of-kind system

I led the complete engineering development of Tutr Hyperloop's first lightweight composite vacuum pressure chamber — a critical subsystem validation asset for testing pod components under realistic low-pressure Hyperloop conditions.

The initial prototype (V1.0) experienced structural failure during pressurization testing. Rather than abandoning the approach, I conducted root-cause analysis using ANSYS Mechanical FEA, identified the failure mechanisms, and executed a systematic redesign through approximately 15 FEA-driven iteration cycles.

The redesigned chamber achieved 1 kPa absolute pressure with stable vacuum performance over an 18-hour hold test — validating both structural integrity and sealing reliability under operational conditions.

Engineering Narrative

Design requirements & V1.0 failure analysis

The chamber needed to be lightweight, structurally reliable, and vacuum-compatible while accommodating electrical and sensor feedthroughs for battery and instrumentation integration. The target was Ø800 mm × 2000 mm internal dimensions with 1 kPa absolute operating pressure.

The first prototype (V1.0) failed during pressurization testing. I conducted a thorough root-cause analysis using ANSYS Mechanical FEA, identifying two primary failure mechanisms:

  • Insufficient laminate thickness in the cylindrical wall section, leading to excessive hoop stress under vacuum loading
  • Stress concentrations at the flange-composite interface, where the aluminum end flanges transferred concentrated loads into the carbon fiber shell

This analysis informed every design decision in the V2.0 redesign.

The V1.0 prototype after structural failure during pressurization testing — the starting point for systematic FEA-driven redesign and root-cause analysis.
Carbon fiber hand lay-up in progress during V2.0 fabrication, showing the optimized laminate sequence with reinforced flange transition zones.

FEA-driven redesign & composite fabrication

The V2.0 redesign addressed both failure modes through systematic ANSYS Mechanical FEA iterations (~15 cycles):

  • Optimized carbon fiber lay-up sequences with increased wall thickness in high-stress regions and tapered transitions to minimize stress concentrations
  • Reinforced flange bonding with graded adhesive layers and mechanical interlocking features to distribute interface loads
  • Integrated aluminum sealing surfaces with precision-machined O-ring grooves for reliable vacuum sealing

I coordinated composite fabrication vendors and supervised hand lay-up, curing schedules, and post-processing workflows to achieve the dimensional tolerances required for reliable vacuum sealing.

Integrated electrical & sensor feedthroughs

A critical challenge was maintaining vacuum integrity while enabling full subsystem connectivity. I developed and integrated multi-pin electrical feedthroughs for two distinct purposes:

  • High-current power delivery for the Linear Induction Motor (LIM) and Electromagnetic (EM) systems — requiring robust conductor sizing and thermal management
  • Sensor signal transmission for temperature, pressure, and strain monitoring — requiring shielded, low-noise pathways

Each feedthrough was designed with double O-ring seals and tested independently before chamber integration to ensure no single point of failure could compromise the vacuum envelope.

Testing & Validation

Extended vacuum validation confirms reliability

The chamber was validated inside Tutr's dedicated 8.6 m vacuum test facility. The test protocol involved:

  • Initial pump-down to target pressure using the facility's vacuum pump system
  • Sealing verification at incremental pressure steps to identify any leak paths
  • Extended 18-hour hold test at 1 kPa absolute to confirm structural stability and sealing reliability over operational durations

Pressure rise remained below 5 kPa over the full 18-hour period, confirming both structural integrity under sustained vacuum loading and the effectiveness of the O-ring sealing system and feedthrough design.

The chamber was subsequently integrated into the Hyperloop pod for subsystem-level testing under realistic low-pressure conditions.

Deployment & Organizational Impact

Establishing a validated pressure vessel workflow

The chamber became Tutr Hyperloop's first operational composite vacuum pressure chamber, establishing a validated workflow for lightweight pressure vessel development that the organization can replicate for future programs.

  • First composite vacuum pressure chamber developed at Tutr Hyperloop
  • Validated at 1 kPa absolute with <5 kPa rise over ~18 hours
  • Integrated electrical & sensor feedthroughs without compromising vacuum integrity
  • Redesigned from V1.0 failure using systematic FEA-driven engineering
  • Enabled subsequent battery and LIM subsystem testing inside the Hyperloop pod
