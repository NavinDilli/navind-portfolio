Overview & Ownership

Turning V1.0 prototype failure into a 1 kPa qualified composite pressure vessel

Executed at IC&SR, IIT Madras (sponsored by Indian Railways under the HyTech Center of Excellence for Hyperloop Technology) from April 2024 to January 2026, I spearheaded the end-to-end engineering development of a ₹22 lakh, Ø800 mm × 2 m carbon-fiber composite vacuum pressure chamber inside the 8.6 m test facility at NCCRD, IIT Madras.

When the initial prototype (V1.0) experienced structural buckling and interface failure during vacuum pump-down, I led the root-cause investigation in ANSYS Mechanical FEA, isolating shell buckling and flange-composite stress concentrations, and drove a systematic redesign across 15 FEA iteration cycles.

The qualified V2.0 vessel achieved 1 kPa absolute vacuum pressure with <5 kPa pressure rise over an 18-hour hold test at ~70 kg total system mass (a 50% weight reduction compared to an equivalent steel vessel).

Engineering Narrative

Design requirements & V1.0 failure root-cause analysis

The vessel was designed to house high-voltage battery packs and power electronics at atmospheric pressure while surrounded by a 1 kPa Hyperloop vacuum environment (or hold internal 1 kPa vacuum during ground qualification), requiring a lightweight Ø800 mm × 2000 mm envelope with hermetic electrical and sensor feedthroughs.

Following the V1.0 structural failure under external pressure differential, I performed nonlinear buckling and composite failure analysis in ANSYS Mechanical, pinpointing two primary failure modes:

  • Insufficient hoop bending stiffness in the cylindrical carbon-fiber shell section, triggering premature shell buckling instability
  • Interlaminar shear and peel stress concentrations at the aluminum-flange-to-composite joint under axial vacuum end-load transfer

These findings governed the 15-iteration V2.0 structural and sealing redesign.

The V1.0 prototype following structural buckling during vacuum testing — the catalyst for 15 iterations of ANSYS Mechanical FEA redesign.
Carbon-fiber laminate lay-up during V2.0 fabrication, incorporating reinforced hoop plies and tapered flange transition zones.

15-iteration FEA redesign & composite manufacturing

Through 15 ANSYS Mechanical FEA iterations, I re-engineered both the composite laminate schedule and the metallic end-ring interfaces:

  • Buckling-resistant carbon-fiber laminate architecture with tailored hoop and axial ply build-ups and tapered drop-offs near the flange boundaries
  • Reinforced aluminum-to-composite bonded joints distributing axial pressure loads without interlaminar peel failure
  • Precision-machined aluminum sealing flanges with FKM (Viton) O-ring grooves engineered for low outgassing and repeatable elastomeric compression

I managed composite and CNC machining vendors through lay-up, curing, flange bonding, and post-cure metrology to hold strict sealing flatness tolerances.

Hermetic high-current & multi-sensor feedthroughs

Operating active Hyperloop subsystems across a 1 kPa pressure boundary required custom hermetic electrical and instrumentation feedthroughs integrated into the chamber bulkhead:

  • High-current power feedthroughs for Linear Induction Motor (LIM) and electromagnetic levitation power buses — sized for thermal dissipation and dielectric isolation
  • Multi-pin instrumentation feedthroughs for thermocouple, pressure, and strain telemetry — routed through shielded low-noise connectors

Each feedthrough assembly incorporated dual FKM O-ring seals and underwent standalone helium/pressure-decay leak verification prior to full chamber integration.

Testing & Validation

18-hour vacuum hold qualification at NCCRD, IIT Madras

The V2.0 composite vessel underwent formal V&V qualification inside the 8.6 m vacuum test tube at NCCRD, IIT Madras:

  • Staged evacuation down to 1 kPa absolute operating pressure while monitoring shell strain and flange seating
  • Hermetic leak-rate audit across both aluminum end-rings and all power/instrumentation feedthrough ports
  • 18-hour static vacuum hold test at 1 kPa absolute to verify structural creep stability and FKM O-ring seal retention

Total pressure rise remained below 5 kPa over 18 hours, qualifying the composite shell, bonded flange joints, and hermetic feedthroughs for active Hyperloop pod subsystem testing.

The qualified V2.0 carbon-fiber vacuum chamber mounted on its support saddle during integration preparation at NCCRD, IIT Madras.
