Overview & Ownership

Building TuTr Hyperloop's first Digital Engineering capability from the ground up

I led a two-member simulation-test correlation cell to develop TuTr Hyperloop's first Structural Digital Twin proof-of-concept — establishing the organization's foundational Digital Engineering capability.

The objective was to demonstrate live Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) by acquiring micro-strain signals from a physical structure using 120 Ω strain gauge Wheatstone bridges, conditioning the signals through an INA101 instrumentation amplifier and NI USB-6003 DAQ hardware, and updating a 3D FEA-correlated virtual model in MATLAB App Designer with sub-100 ms latency.

This proof-of-concept became the architectural template for TuTr's broader Digital Engineering ecosystem — directly informing technical architecture proposals for a multi-crore national rail Digital Twin initiative, live magnetic levitation chassis SHM testing, an embedded 12V Battery SOC/SOH Digital Twin, and 1D Simscape electro-thermal cooling models.

The complete Digital Twin workstation — physical 300 mm steel cantilever scale, power supply, NI USB-6003 DAQ, INA101 instrumentation amplifier, and live MATLAB dashboard monitor.
02

Engineering Narrative

Precision instrumentation for strain acquisition

The physical benchmark was a 300 mm steel cantilever scale instrumented with 120 Ω strain gauges (implemented across Full, Half, and Quarter Wheatstone bridge configurations for temperature compensation, axial/bending isolation, and high sensitivity).

Strain gauge bonding followed strict surface preparation protocols: degreasing, abrasion, neutralization, and cyanoacrylate curing under controlled pressure to ensure repeatable strain transfer and electrical continuity across loading cycles.

An INA101 instrumentation amplifier was used for precision low-level differential signal conditioning, providing high common-mode rejection ratio (CMRR) and calibrated gain before digitization. The conditioned output was routed through analog low-pass filtering circuitry prior to DAQ acquisition.

120 Ω Wheatstone bridge strain gauge installation bonded to the 300 mm steel cantilever scale, providing temperature-compensated, high-sensitivity bending strain measurement.

Data acquisition & signal chain architecture

The complete signal chain was engineered for minimal noise and high measurement fidelity:

  • 120 Ω strain gauges (implemented across Full, Half, and Quarter Wheatstone bridge configurations for temperature compensation, axial/bending isolation, and high sensitivity) detect micro-strain under applied loads
  • INA101 instrumentation amplifier provides precision differential amplification with 60+ dB CMRR
  • Passive RC filtering on the analog front-end suppresses high-frequency switching noise and 50 Hz mains hum
  • NI USB-6003 DAQ digitizes analog channels at 16-bit resolution with software-configurable sampling rates
  • MATLAB Data Acquisition Toolbox streams continuous telemetry frames in real time over USB

The entire analog front-end was shielded and single-point grounded to prevent ground loops, essential for resolving microvolt-level bridge imbalances.

Real-time visualization & FEA correlation

I developed the MATLAB App Designer dashboard to provide a comprehensive live view of the structural state:

  • Raw voltage display — live streaming of conditioned bridge output for diagnostics
  • Physical load estimation — calibrated inverse conversion from bridge voltage to applied tip force/mass
  • 3D stress field visualization — color-mapped compression and tension contours rendered on the virtual cantilever geometry
  • Max yield stress indicator — real-time margin tracking against material yield limits

The 3D stress field was computed using a reduced-order compliance matrix derived from ANSYS Mechanical FEA — pre-computing nodal stress distributions for unit load cases and superposing them in real time from measured strain. This architecture achieved a <100 ms update rate while preserving 3D FEA accuracy.

Testing & Validation

Three-way correlation validates the Digital Twin

I validated the Digital Twin through progressive calibrated loading across full, half, and quarter 120 Ω Wheatstone bridge arrangements:

  • Known calibration masses were applied at measured moment arms to induce controlled bending stress
  • Live dashboard telemetry — conditioned bridge voltage, inverse-estimated load, and peak von Mises / bending stress — was logged in real time
  • Measured values were benchmarked against both analytical Euler-Bernoulli beam theory and independent ANSYS Mechanical FEA models

The real-time 3D stress field visualization tracked transient load steps in under 100 ms, with inverse load and peak stress predictions agreeing closely with analytical and FEA baselines.

This three-way verification workflow — physical strain telemetry, analytical mechanics, and numerical FEA — provided the engineering foundation to scale into full subsystem Digital Twins.

Expanded Digital Engineering Ecosystem

Pipeline 1: Levitation Chassis SHM & Embedded 12V Battery SOC/SOH Digital Twin

Scaling the cantilever SHM benchmark to Hyperloop subsystems required a unified hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) telemetry and state-estimation pipeline spanning structural and electrochemical domains:

  • Levitation Chassis SHM & Virtual Load Sensing: Live 120 Ω strain gauge bridges (Full, Half, and Quarter Wheatstone topologies) and INA101 amplifiers feed the 16-bit NI USB-6003 DAQ at <100 ms latency. Automated Python (PyAnsys) scripts run parametric static structural FEA across multi-axis magnetic levitation load cases to extract the strain-to-load compliance matrix, enabling real-time inverse force estimation and optimal gauge placement on the levitation chassis.
  • Embedded 12V Battery Diagnostics Digital Twin (SOC/SOH): A Raspberry Pi 5 acquires high-resolution pack current, terminal voltage, and shunt power over I²C from INA226 sensors, streaming live telemetry into a MATLAB/Simulink equivalent-circuit battery model with Extended Kalman Filtering (EKF) for real-time State of Charge (SOC) and State of Health (SOH) tracking.

Pipeline 2: 1D Simscape Electro-Thermal Cooling & PyAnsys Automation Workflow

To prevent thermal derating in high-power Linear Induction Motor (LIM) propulsion windings and optimize Metro Rail HVAC subsystems, I developed a multi-fidelity 3D-to-1D co-simulation workflow:

  • 3D ANSYS Fluent CHT Characterization: High-fidelity 3D Conjugate Heat Transfer (CHT) simulations characterize cold-plate and cooling-channel convective heat transfer coefficients $h(\dot{m})$ and hydraulic pressure drops $\Delta P(\dot{m})$ across operating Reynolds numbers.
  • 1D MATLAB/Simulink & Simscape Thermo-Fluid Plant: Extracted $h(\dot{m})$ and $\Delta P(\dot{m})$ lookup tables calibrate a fast-executing 1D Simscape thermal-liquid network coupled with $I^2R$ copper/core loss injection.
  • Closed-Loop PID Thermal Control: A PID controller dynamically modulates coolant pump flow rate to maintain LIM stator windings safely below Class H (180°C) insulation limits during peak thrust profiles while minimizing auxiliary pump power.
